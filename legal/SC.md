## 1. Wholesaling / licensing / disclosures
| # | Rule | Source | Checked | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **2024 Act No. 204 (H.4754), eff. 2024-05-21** rewrote the Real Estate Practice Act. S.C. Code 40-57-30(44): "Wholesaling" = having a contractual interest to buy residential real estate from an owner, then **marketing the property** to a different buyer before taking legal ownership; advertising/marketing real estate owned by another with expectation of compensation "falls under the definition of broker and requires licensure." Assigning or offering to assign a contractual right is not, by itself, wholesaling. | https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 1.2 | 40-57-135(E)(1): an ad that markets only a **contractual position**, without implying/marketing the underlying property, is permissible. | same | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 1.3 | **SC Real Estate Commission Advisory Opinion (2024-11-14):** marketing a contract while not revealing the property is "practically impossible"; any photo, address, TMS, beds/baths, condition, neighborhood, etc. = marketing the property = wholesaling = license required. Marketing includes private email groups, Facebook groups and verbal communication. Compliance is "very limited" (e.g., being approached by a buyer without any marketing). | https://www.llr.sc.gov/re/News/Wholesaling-Assignment-of-Contracts-Guidance.pdf | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 1.4 | 40-57-350(A): licensed brokerages and their agents may not engage in or assist wholesaling (so a "licensed partner" must structure it as normal brokerage or a double close, not wholesaling). | https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 1.5 | No statutory seller-disclosure form or cooling-off right for wholesale contracts found. | — | 2026-11-19 | UNVERIFIED |
| 1.6 | SC closings generally must be supervised by an SC-licensed attorney (UPL case law). | — | — | UNVERIFIED |
