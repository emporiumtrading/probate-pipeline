## 1. Wholesaling, licensing, required disclosures, cancellation

| # | Rule (what it appears to say) | Source | Checked | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **R.C. 5301.95 (Senate Bill 155, 136th GA), effective 2026-03-02.** A "wholesaler" is anyone who, for a fee or expected consideration, enters a purchase contract for **residential real property (1–4 dwelling units)** as grantee and **assigns or novates** it (or, as grantor, assigns/novates without holding legal title). Exemptions only: assignment to a blood relative (individuals), or to a parent/affiliate/subsidiary under common control. | https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5301.95 | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 1.2 | **Mandatory separate disclosure before any binding contract.** The wholesaler (as grantee) must give the **record owner** a conspicuous written disclosure, **separate from the purchase contract**, in **boldface, ≥ 12-point font**, "in substantially the following form" (statutory text quoted in "Required contract disclosures" below). **Both the owner and wholesaler must sign and date it before** the binding contract is made; the wholesaler "shall not enter into a binding contract" until that happens. | same | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 1.3 | **Cancellation right if the disclosure is missing:** owner may cancel **at any time before close of escrow, without penalty**; escrow/closing agent must disburse the wholesaler's earnest money **to the owner** within 30 days. If the disclosure was properly given, the statute creates **no** separate cooling-off period. | same, (C)(1) | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |

| 1.10 | City/county add-ons (Columbus / Franklin County): none found. | searched 2026-11-19 | 2026-11-19 | UNVERIFIED |

**Fee-splitting:** Paying a "bird dog" or another wholesaler for finding/negotiating a deal may make *them* unlicensed brokers (4735.01(A)(7) "procuring of prospects") and is not collectible by them (4735.21). Paying a licensed agent a referral fee outside their brokerage is generally barred by license law. **Do not propose any fee split, JV split, or referral payment to anyone until the attorney approves the structure.** (Statute VERIFIED; application UNVERIFIED.)

## 2. Probate sale authority (who can sign, approval, price floors)

| # | Rule | Source | Checked | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Will with power of sale:** executor may sell without a probate court order ("no order shall be required"), for any purpose in the estate's best interest unless the will limits it. No statutory price floor — but fiduciary duty applies (a lowball contract can be challenged by heirs). | https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2113.39 ; https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2127.01 | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 2.2 | **No will power of sale → "consent power of sale" (R.C. 2127.011):** executor/administrator may sell privately only if **the surviving spouse and ALL legatees/devisees (testate) or ALL heirs (intestate) give written consent**, filed with the probate court, **and the price is at least 80% of the appraised value in the approved inventory.** Not available if the spouse or any heir/devisee is a **minor**. | https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2127.011 | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 2.3 | **Otherwise: court land-sale proceeding (R.C. 2127.02 et seq.).** If personal property can't pay debts, the fiduciary must file a civil action in probate court for authority to sell. Under a court order, a **private sale may not be for less than 100% of the appraised value**; a public auction may not go below **2/3** of appraisal (improved) or 1/2 (unimproved) unless the court re-fixes the price/re-appraises (R.C. 2127.33). Sale must be **returned to and confirmed by the court**, which then orders the deed (R.C. 2127.35). Public sales need 3 weeks of newspaper notice (R.C. 2127.32). | https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2127.02 ; .../section-2127.32 ; .../section-2127.33 ; .../section-2127.35 | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 2.4 | **Heirs taking title instead (certificate of transfer, R.C. 2113.61):** if the property is distributed rather than sold, the court issues a certificate of transfer to the heirs/devisees, and then **every heir on the certificate must sign** the deed/contract. | https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2113.61 | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 2.5 | **Creditor period:** claims must be presented within **6 months after death** (R.C. 2117.06(B)); late claims are barred, with exceptions in 2117.061 (e.g., Medicaid estate recovery notice rules). Distributions made early expose the fiduciary to personal liability (R.C. 2113.53). Expect fiduciaries/title to resist closing before the 6-month window or before debts are clear. | https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2117.06 ; https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2113.53 | 2026-11-19 | VERIFIED |
| 2.6 | **Medicaid estate recovery** (Ohio AG / R.C. 5162.21) may assert a claim against the estate, including the house. | not fetched | — | UNVERIFIED |
| 2.7 | **Letters required:** only a court-appointed executor/administrator with letters of authority (or all heirs after a certificate of transfer) can sign. A family member "handling things" without letters cannot bind the estate. | general probate practice; Franklin County Probate Court https://probate.franklincountyohio.gov | — | UNVERIFIED |

**Deal impact:** The 80% (consent sale) and 100% (court-ordered private sale) appraisal floors are measured against the **inventory appraisal**, which is often near the county auditor value. If MAO (70% of ARV minus repairs minus fee) is below 80% of the inventory appraisal, the deal **cannot** be done by consent sale and needs either a will power of sale, a court-ordered re-appraisal, or heirs taking title first. Deal Analyst must compare MAO to 0.8 × inventory value on every Ohio lead.

**5301.95 in probate:** The statute requires the disclosure to the "**record owner**." In an estate the record owner may still be the decedent; who signs the disclosure (executor? every heir? consenting spouse?) is unclear. Safe default until the attorney answers: have the fiduciary **and** every consenting spouse/heir sign the disclosure.

## 4. Tax & entity (questions for a CPA — UNVERIFIED general points)

- Assignment fees are usually ordinary business income; self-employment tax likely applies to an individual/single-member LLC.
- Ohio has municipal income tax (Columbus city income tax) on net profits — ask whether it applies to assignment fees and to which city.
- Ohio Commercial Activity Tax (CAT) has a gross-receipts exclusion threshold — ask if it could ever apply.
- Ohio real property conveyance fee is charged on the deed; ask who pays it and whether a double close triggers it twice.
- Entity (LLC), general-liability insurance, quarterly estimates, 1099s from title, records of marketing/skip-trace/data costs and lost earnest money.

## Blocking issues (stop until answered)

1. **5301.95 disclosure form must be in hand, attorney-approved, and signed by the record owner(s) and wholesaler BEFORE any purchase contract is signed.** No Ohio contract goes out without it. (A missing form = seller can cancel up to closing and keep our earnest money, plus CSPA liability.)
2. **Who is the "record owner" for the disclosure in an estate** (executor, all heirs, spouse)? Get the attorney's answer before the first Ohio contract.
3. **Sale authority + price floor per lead:** confirm whether the will gives a power of sale; if not, the price must be **≥ 80% of the inventory appraisal** with all heirs' written consent (no minors), or **≥ 100%** in a court-ordered private sale with court confirmation. Any lead where MAO < 80% of inventory value is blocked for a consent sale.
4. **No public advertising of the house** (photos, address, beds/baths) to find a buyer without a license or licensed partner — market only to a private buyers list, pending attorney/ODRE confirmation.
5. **No fee splits** with bird dogs, other wholesalers, or agents until the attorney approves.

## Required contract disclosures

**Separate page, boldface, at least 12-point font, signed and dated by owner and wholesaler BEFORE the purchase contract (R.C. 5301.95(B)) — statutory text, "substantially the following form":**

> **"Ohio law requires a wholesaler acting as a grantee, before entering into a contract or agreement that conveys an interest in residential real property, to provide certain information to the record owner in a conspicuous manner printed in boldface type in a font size not less than twelve points. Failure by a wholesaler to present or complete this form is an unfair or deceptive act or practice. Any person who enters into an agreement that conveys an interest in residential real property to a wholesaler acting as a grantee without receiving this disclosure has a cause of action against the wholesaler. A wholesaler acting as a grantee is prohibited from entering into a binding contract to acquire an interest in residential real property unless this statement is signed and dated by the record owner of the property.**
>
> **The owner acknowledges that the person presenting this document is a wholesaler, as defined by section 5301.95 of the Revised Code, and that all buyers and sellers of real estate are entitled to seek legal or professional advice before entering into any agreement or contract regarding the purchase or sale of property, including an agreement with a wholesaler. A wholesaler is acting on the wholesaler's own behalf and does not represent the owner in this transaction. A wholesaler enters assignable contracts with owners and seeks to sell or assign the wholesaler's interest for a profit. The wholesaler may assign the wholesaler's interest in the purchase contract to a third party without the owner's consent before closing. The wholesaler may charge a fee to the third-party buyer separately for profit. The agreed purchase price between the owner and wholesaler may be below market value and is conveyed voluntarily.**
>
> **The owner acknowledges disclosure of the information provided in this form by signing and dating below:**
> **_______________ (Property owner signature) ______ (date)**
> **_______________ (Wholesaler signature) _____ (date)"**

(Source: https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5301.95, VERIFIED 2026-11-19. Have the attorney confirm final wording and signers.)

Also in the purchase agreement (attorney to draft): "and/or assigns" language; probate-court/consent contingency (2127.011 consent filed, or court confirmation under 2127.35); inspection/exit period and earnest-money terms; as-is; personal-property clause; access for buyer showings; fiduciary signs "as Executor/Administrator of the Estate of ___, Case No. ___"; no memorandum of contract recorded without attorney sign-off. Separate assignment agreement with end buyer (non-refundable deposit, non-circumvention).
