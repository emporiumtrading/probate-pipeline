## What we do
We find estates in probate that own real property, write to the personal representative (or the estate's attorney, when one is listed), and offer to buy the property as-is for cash with no fees or repairs for the estate. We sign a purchase contract with the estate, then either assign that contract to a vetted cash buyer for a fee or buy and resell at a simultaneous closing. The title company holds all earnest money and handles closing. We disclose our intent to assign in writing, we never call or text without the legal basis to do so, and we honor every stop request the same day.

## Blocking issues (stop until answered)

1. **Written equitable-interest disclosures (Prop. Code §5.0205 / Occ. Code §1101.0045)** must be attorney-approved and given **before** contracting: (a) to the **owner/seller** that we intend to assign the contract; (b) to **every potential buyer** that we are assigning a contract interest and do not hold legal title. Missing disclosure = unlicensed brokerage.
2. **Market only the contract (equitable interest)** to a private buyers list; no public ads of the house as if we own it, pending attorney review of TREC "truth in advertising" position.
3. **Signing authority per lead:** letters issued? independent vs. dependent administration? muniment of title (all devisees sign)? surviving spouse homestead right? No contract before letters (or all devisees) exist.
4. **No memorandum of contract recorded** (HB 4063 / Prop. Code §12.020).
5. **No calls or texts** until SB 140 / Bus. & Com. Code ch. 302 applicability is answered; mail only.
6. **No fee splits** until the attorney approves.

## Required contract disclosures

No statutory wording is prescribed. Draft (attorney to approve), on the purchase agreement **and** as a separate signed acknowledgment, delivered before signing:

> **Notice to Seller (Tex. Prop. Code §5.0205; Tex. Occ. Code §1101.0045):** Buyer is a real estate investor, is not a licensed real estate broker or sales agent, and does not represent Seller. Buyer intends to assign Buyer's interest in this contract (or sell an option) to another buyer before closing, and may be paid a fee for that assignment. Seller may wish to consult an attorney before signing.

> **Notice to potential buyers / assignees (Tex. Prop. Code §5.0205):** The assignor is selling only an interest in a contract to purchase [address] (an equitable interest) and **does not have legal title** to the property.

Also in the purchase agreement (attorney to draft): "and/or assigns"; signer capacity ("as Independent Executor of the Estate of ___, Deceased, Cause No. ___, Probate Court No. _ of Harris County") and copy of letters; for dependent administration a court-order/approval contingency; for muniment of title, all devisees sign; homestead / surviving-spouse occupancy representation; inspection/exit period and earnest money held by title; as-is; personal-property clause; access for buyer walk-throughs; no memorandum recorded. Consider TREC promulgated forms only through an attorney (TREC forms are for license holders). Separate assignment agreement with end buyer (non-refundable deposit, non-circumvention).
