# Dynamic messaging (no one-size-fits-all)

Letters, emails, offers and deal sheets are **composed per lead** from the facts in its files, not filled into one fixed template. Only the legally required wording is fixed (state disclosures, assignment disclosure, opt-out). The attorney approves those fixed blocks and the contract templates once per state; everything around them adapts.

## Inputs to read before writing
From `research.md`, `plan.md` and the comms log:
- Who you're writing to: executor (relative or professional fiduciary), estate attorney, heir-owner after a certificate of transfer, or co-owner.
- Situation signals: out-of-state representative · several heirs (agreeing or not) · tenant or relative living there · vacant / utilities off / code issues · mortgage or back taxes · court approval needed or price floor · deadline pressure (creditor period, taxes, insurance) · how long since death · prior contacts and what they said.
- Their likely priorities: speed, certainty, no cleanout, no showings, fairness among heirs, getting the court process done, privacy.
- Channel: mail, email to attorney (professional, concise), or reply in an existing thread (match their tone and length).

## Compose
1. **Opening**: condolence only when writing to family, and only on first contact. Attorneys get a direct, professional opening.
2. **Why we're writing**: one sentence naming the property and that we buy as-is for cash and may assign the contract.
3. **What we solve for them**: pick the 2–3 that match their signals (e.g. out-of-state → "we handle cleanout, utilities and access, and close by mail"; several heirs → "one simple offer you can share with everyone, with our numbers shown"; court sale → "we'll time closing to the court's approval and can meet the appraisal requirement"; tenant → "we buy with the tenant in place").
4. **Offer or next step**: first contact invites a conversation. Later messages may carry a specific offer within authority, with the reasoning (comps and repairs summary) attached for credibility.
5. **Required blocks**: the investor/assignment disclosure, the state-specific wording, and the opt-out line.
6. **Easy no**: a sentence that makes declining easy.

## Variation
- Each follow-up must add something new (updated comps, a solved obstacle, a different term), never a repeat.
- Vary length: letter ≤ 1 page, email ≤ 150 words, replies mirror theirs.
- Plain language, no hype, no investor jargon ("MAO", "ARV", "assignment fee") in messages to families.
- Keep it honest. Maximum profit comes from finding terms the estate values (speed, as-is, certainty) so the price can be lower, not from misleading anyone.

The Compliance Officer checks every composed message before it goes out.
